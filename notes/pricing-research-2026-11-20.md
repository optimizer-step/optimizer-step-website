Why Optimizer Step's price ladder looks the way it does. Collected on 20 November 2026 by research agents
from official pages; a second, skeptical agent re-opened every source and confirmed each price before it
went into these tables (77 items checked, 74 verified). Prices change often: re-check before relying on them.

The owner chose aggressive penetration pricing ("very cheap compared to the competition"). The research
alone suggested a higher ladder (₹1,999 / ₹3,999 / ₹4,999 / ₹7,999–9,999); see the evidence section.
Founding 100: All Tracks free, the Pro upgrade for ₹1,799 (40% off the ₹3,000 step) and 40% off future paid tracks.

Expect freemium conversion in the low single digits (3–5% of free users in six months is typical; 6–8% is
great), so plan for tens to low hundreds of paid learners in the first year at today's audience size.

## Rules the pricing must follow in India

- No false urgency, scarcity or popularity (CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023; CCPA coaching-sector guidelines 2024).
  The founding-seat counter must stay real, and "Best value" is used instead of an unproven "Most popular".
- "Free" must be honest: say up front what stays free and what is paid, disclose any required sign-up, never
  require card details or autopay for the free course, and never call an included bonus "free" inside a paid tier.
  (CCPA fined PhysicsWallah ₹5 lakh on 3 June 2026 for gated "free" courses and a pre-selected donation.)
- Show one all-inclusive price with a tax breakup at checkout, list payment methods and how to cancel anything
  recurring, and take consent only by an active click (Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020).
- From 1 January 2027, an announced price cut must show the prior price, meaning the lowest price in the previous
  30 days, and sellers must run a yearly dark-pattern self-audit and display the certificate (E-Commerce Amendment
  Rules 2026). So the site says "Free for the first 100; ₹1,999 after that" instead of striking out a price that
  was never charged.
- No job or salary guarantees, disclaimers in the same font as the claim, and written consent for testimonials
  (CCPA coaching-sector guidelines 2024).
- Payments: UPI first (85.5% of digital payment volume); card EMI only on the top tier, and never call an
  interest-bearing EMI "No Cost" (RBI card directions 2025).

Takeaways from this segment:
- Price bands (Nov 2026). Low-ticket: ₹299–₹3,300 (CampusX notes ₹299–₹499; ₹399 Udemy courses from Chai aur Code and Krish Naik; Codebasics single courses ₹900–₹3,300; CodeWithHarry Data Science ₹2,899). Core programs: ₹3,999–₹12,999 (100xDevs single track ₹3,999; Apna Prime 3.0 ₹6,860; ChaiCode cohort ₹7,999; Krish Naik live bootcamps ₹10,000–₹12,000; CampusX DSMP ₹10,600; Sheryians ₹12,999 + GST). All-access plans: ₹6,650–₹21,000 (Euron Plus, CampusX One, Krish Naik Pro). Premium high-touch: ₹18,000–₹60,000 (Codebasics bootcamp and live cohorts, PW Pro, Euron Super 30 ₹59,000). Optimizer Step's ₹4,999 for all four tracks sits at the bottom of the core band. It is below every flagship from the 1M+ channels and close to 100xDevs' all-tracks bundle (₹5,999), which suits a channel of about 4.4k subscribers.
- The Apple-style good/better/best ladder is already normal among the larger creators: CampusX One ₹9,999 / ₹14,999 / ₹19,999 with the middle plan badged 'Most Popular'; PW Skills Basic / Premium / Pro; Apna Prime ₹6,860 vs the Sigma + Prime bundle ₹10,400; one Krish Naik bootcamp at ₹8,000–₹12,000 vs ₹21,000/yr all-access. At CampusX, 100xDevs and Apna each step up costs about ₹2,000–₹5,000 (+33–50%). Every ladder shows an anchor: strike-through list prices, 'buying separately costs ₹42,000+', '₹87,999+ · Save 76%', or per-month framing (₹1,750/mo, ₹554/mo).
- The closest match to a four-track catalogue is 100xDevs. Each single track (AI & ML, Web Dev + DevOps, Web3) is ₹3,999, but all three plus a free DSA track cost ₹5,999: +₹2,000 for three times the content, so the single tracks mainly act as the decoy. Applied to Optimizer Step: a single paid track at about ₹1,999–₹2,499 (in line with Codebasics ₹1,200–₹2,700 and CodeWithHarry ₹2,899) makes 'All 4 tracks' at ₹4,999 the obvious choice, because two single tracks already cost about the same.
- Higher tiers rarely add new content; they add time, people and career support. Examples: longer validity (CampusX 1 year vs 2 years; DSMP 3 years; ChaiCode 730 days; Apna 15 months, explicitly not lifetime), notes and resources (CampusX notes 'worth ₹3,000'), live sessions and community (CampusX Insider, Krish Naik Pro, Codebasics bi-weekly live), and 1:1 mentorship, resume/interview prep and job help (PW Pro, Codebasics, Euron Super 30). This is the equivalent of Apple's storage and AppleCare upsell. A top 'Pro / Mentored' tier at about ₹7,999–₹9,999 (live doubt sessions, project reviews, 2-year access, certificate, community) would sit on the ChaiCode (₹7,999), Apna (₹6,860), CampusX Learner (₹9,999) and Krish Naik (₹10,000) price points. It also anchors ₹4,999 as the sensible middle choice.
- Upgrade credits, like a trade-in, remove the risk of buying the wrong tier. Codebasics deducts money already paid for individual courses from the ₹18,000 bootcamp ('pay only the difference') and even suggests buying Python, then Math, then the bootcamp as a do-it-yourself instalment plan. Its live cohorts credit past purchases too, and CampusX has a loyalty-discount calculator based on past spend. Optimizer Step can say: 'whatever you paid for a single track counts toward All 4 tracks / Pro'.
- Free entry is universal, and the strongest versions capture an account and end with a paid next step. Euron's free courses include certificates but need a free login ('no subscription, no card'). CampusX gives away full courses (MLflow, Fine-tuning Transformers, Object Detection) and sells notes for its free YouTube playlists at ₹299–₹499 (₹1,099 bundle). Codebasics lists free courses and projects beside ₹900–₹3,300 starter courses. Krish Naik shows 14 free demo-class recordings before a ₹12,000 bootcamp. ChaiCode's FAQ tells cash-strapped students to start free on YouTube. A free ML Foundations track with a certificate and login fits these norms; its last module should hand off to the paid tracks, and paid notes or a cheap single track can serve as a micro-step.
- Show one all-in INR price. Most creators show GST-inclusive totals: Apna ('18% GST already included'), Krish Naik ('incl. GST'), Codebasics ('Incl. of all taxes'), ChaiCode (final ₹7,999). Sheryians adds '+GST', and CampusX's store shows pre-GST figures (₹8,983.05 = ₹10,600), which is confusing. Refund windows range from 'until the first class' (ChaiCode) to 7 days minus ₹899 (CampusX) to 30 days full refund (Codebasics). EMI is avoided at these price points (ChaiCode discourages loans; Codebasics has no EMI on self-paced bootcamps), and annual plans are framed per month.
- Scarcity and launch pricing are standard: Sheryians' 'Early Bird Discount' (₹19,999 cut to ₹12,999) and 'First Time Offer'; Codebasics' waitlists and 'inner circle' price (₹45,000 until 1 Dec, then ₹60,000); Euron Super 30 capped at 30 seats; Apna's Diwali offer of up to 30% off. Optimizer Step's 'first 100 founding students free' fits this pattern. After those seats go, keep ₹4,999 as the visible list price and run short, time-boxed launch offers rather than lowering the list price.

- Market price map (Nov 2026, INR). Free: Simplilearn SkillUp, upGrad, Scaler Topics, and Great Learning Academy content. Single self-paced courses: ₹600-₹3,300 (Codebasics) and ₹1,999 (GfG). Optimizer Step's planned ₹4,999 for all four tracks sits between GfG's ₹1,999 single course and PW Skills' ₹6,999 Basic tier, at the low end of the full-programme band. That suits a 4.4k-subscriber creator brand.
- The Apple-style step-up is already the main tier design in this market. There is a large format jump from self-paced to live, then only a small step to the 'career' tier, which is the only one with job help. PW Skills goes ₹6,999, then ₹34,999 (5x), then ₹39,999 (+14%), and its FAQ says Basic and Premium include no job assistance. upGrad goes ₹30,000, then ₹70,000, then ₹80,000 (+14%), where the top tier is 'Everything in' the middle one plus 1:1 mock interviews, resume and portfolio review, a recommendation letter, and 6 months of career-portal access. PW also shows higher ratings and learner counts on higher tiers (4.5★/5,998+, then 4.7★/7,549+, then 4.9★/10,989+). To copy this, Optimizer Step's top tier should cost only about 15-25% more than the middle tier and be the only one with outcome extras (1:1 mentorship, mock interviews, portfolio and GitHub review).
- Free tiers work as lead capture with a visible ceiling. Simplilearn SkillUp, upGrad and Scaler Topics give free certificates but state that projects, mentorship and placement are paid-only. Great Learning gives free content but charges for the certificate. Takeaways from this segment:
- What the global platforms charge for is practice and proof, not videos. On DeepLearning.AI, the Free plan (₹0) includes every course video, the community and limited labs. Pro (₹999/month or ₹9,990/year) adds graded assignments, quizzes, saved progress, certificates and offline downloads. Others draw the line similarly: Coursera's first module of nearly every course is a free preview (since Sep 2025), DataCamp's first chapter of every course is free, and Kaggle Learn, Google MLCC, fast.ai and the Hugging Face course are fully free. For Optimizer Step, the free entry course should be a complete, genuinely useful course (videos plus notebooks). The paid tiers should then unlock outcomes: graded projects with feedback, a certificate, progress tracking, doubt support, and portfolio or interview prep.
- Every platform uses an Apple-style menu: a badged middle or annual option framed as a per-month price, next to a weaker single-item option. Coursera shows three columns: Individual at ₹1,699/month; Coursera Plus, badged 'Best value', at ₹7,499/year with ₹13,999 struck through, 'Save 46%' and 'less than ₹625 per month'; and Teams. - Price bands seen from India on 20 Nov 2026: (a) one Udemy AI/ML course effectively costs ₹449 to ₹609; the ₹3,089 to ₹4,229 list prices are anchors behind permanent 85 to 86% 'sales'. (c) Paying monthly with no commitment costs ₹1,776 to ₹2,099 a month. Present it the way these platforms do: under ₹417/month over a year, or ₹1,250 per track, with the sum of the single-track prices struck through beside it.

What it means for the ladder:

- Copy Apple's ladder mechanics, not its price levels. On apple.com/in (20 Nov 2026), steps are fixed and predictable: the first storage doubling on the iPhone 18 Pro adds ₹25,000 (about 15%), and moving from Pro to Pro Max adds ₹15,000 (about 9%). An upgraded lower model sits within about ₹5,000 of the next model's base (Air 512GB ₹1,74,900 vs Pro Max 256GB ₹1,79,900). iCloud+ pairs a free rung with a tiny first paid step (₹75/month) and better per-GB value higher up. CIRP finds 46% of US iPhone buyers still pay to step up. For Optimizer Step this means: free starter course → single-track rung → all-four-tracks bundle with a sharply lower per-track price → premium rung that adds services. Each rung should add one clearly countable benefit.
- Make the all-four-tracks bundle the middle 'target' tier. Middle options gain share (Simonson & Tversky), so a premium tier above the bundle acts as its anchor. A near-dominated real option beside it, for example 2 tracks for only about ₹1,000 less, makes the bundle look obviously better. Decoys mainly push buyers upward (Heath & Chatterjee) and work best on unfamiliar buyers when options sit side by side with numeric attributes (Huber, Payne & Puto). In real online sales only 11-25% of shoppers noticed the dominance, but those who did were 1.8-3.2x more likely to buy the dominant option, for +14.3% gross profit (Wu & Cosguner). So put tracks, hours, projects, mentor sessions, months of access and a per-track price in one table. Label the target 'Best value', which can be verified, not 'Most popular' unless sales prove it: false popularity is a specified dark pattern.
- Price-point hypothesis, to check against competitor data. Indian mass-market online learners pay modest amounts: PhysicsWallah batches start around ₹2,000, and its online collections average ₹3,810-3,918 per paying user per period. Indian shoppers read 999-type endings as deals and the next round number as expensive (Kumar & Pandey). A consistent ladder:
- Free starter course.
- 1 track for ₹1,999.
- 2 tracks for ₹3,999, as the near-decoy.
- All 4 tracks for ₹4,999, the target tier. This keeps the publicly stated price and works out to about ₹1,250 per track against ₹1,999 bought singly.
- All 4 plus mentorship, project reviews and live doubt sessions at about ₹7,999-9,999, or an even 'premium' figure.
Build the premium rung the way PW builds Batch Infinity: bundle add-ons that are also sold separately, so the saving is real and visible.
- Expect low single-digit conversion and earn it through engagement. Self-serve freemium typically converts 3-5% of users in six months (great is 6-8%; Lenny/OpenView, mostly SaaS). Duolingo has about 8.6% of monthly users paying. PhysicsWallah's newer verticals show roughly 4-7% paid learners relative to YouTube subscribers, and PW explicitly puts long-term engagement before monetisation. With about 4,390 subscribers, plan for tens to low hundreds of paid learners in year one. Make the free course substantial enough to prove teaching quality, and track free-learner-to-paid conversion for each rung separately from the 100 founding learners. Keep the top tier distinct: Duolingo is sunsetting its top Max tier after moving its headline feature down into Super.
- Payments: make the path UPI-first, with EMI only at the top. UPI carries 85.5% of India's digital payment volume, with an average ticket of ₹1,313, while credit cards are only about 10% of the 115 crore cards outstanding. So the free → ₹1,999 → ₹4,999 path should be one-tap UPI, and card EMI should be offered only on the premium rung. Apple India shows a per-month price ('From ₹26,316/mo.', No Cost EMI over 3 or 6 months) next to the MRP. If you copy that framing, absorb the interest yourself and keep the total visible: RBI's 2025 card directions bar calling an interest-bearing EMI 'No Cost'. Never require card details or autopay to start the free course; the dark-pattern rules list that as a subscription trap.
- Design the free course to survive the PhysicsWallah precedent. On 3 Jun 2026 the CCPA fined PW ₹5 lakh for 'free' courses gated behind a mobile number and email without disclosure, a pre-selected ₹10 donation, and guilt-trip messages. In practice:
- State up front exactly what is free forever and what is paid; advertising 'free' without saying that continued use needs payment counts as drip pricing.
- Disclose any mandatory sign-up, or drop it.
- Keep checkout add-ons unticked and decline buttons neutral.
- Never lock content a buyer already paid for to force an upgrade.
- Inside paid tiers, call bonuses 'included', not 'FREE'; the 2022 free-claim rules forbid calling part of a package free when it is in the package price.
- Price display and strikethrough prices. Under the E-Commerce Rules 2020, show each tier as one all-inclusive figure with a tax breakup; list payment methods, charges and how to cancel any recurring payment; and record consent only through an affirmative click. From 1 Jan 2027, any announced price cut must show the prior price, defined as the lowest price in the previous 30 days. Sellers must also run a yearly dark-pattern self-audit and display the compliance certificate. So describe the launch as 'Free for the first 100 founding learners; ₹4,999 after that', not as a '₹4,999 → FREE' strikethrough for a price that was never charged.
- Urgency and outcome claims. The CCPA coaching guidelines cover all advertising by anyone engaged in coaching, which includes imparting education and study programmes. They ban false urgency or scarcity, false claims about fees or refund policy, and job or salary guarantees. They also require disclaimers in the same font as the claim and written consent for testimonials, taken after the learner's success. CCPA has fined 31 institutes more than ₹1.39 crore. 'First 100 founding seats' is a legitimate scarcity claim only if the cap is real and any seat counter is truthful. Avoid timers that reset, fake 'X people viewing' notices, and 'get a ₹X LPA AI job' promises on every tier.
