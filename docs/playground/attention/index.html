How this attention map works

In a transformer, each word looks at the other words and decides how much attention to pay to each one. This lab shows the core of that idea with a simplified model.

1. Words become vectors

Each word becomes a small vector of numbers. Here the vectors come from the word's letters, which keeps the lab fast and predictable. Real models learn these embeddings from data.

2. Similarity becomes a score

The selected word (the query) is compared with every word using a dot product. Similar vectors get higher scores.

3. Softmax turns scores into weights

Softmax converts the scores into weights that add up to 100%. The sharpness slider scales the scores first: higher sharpness focuses attention on the best match, lower spreads it out.

Real transformers use separate learned query, key and value vectors and many attention heads, but the central step, similarity followed by softmax, is the same.
